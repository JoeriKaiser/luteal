# Phase Guidance and Condition-Aware Research Catalog

## Purpose

This document provides the clinical and human evidence base for phase-aware, condition-aware daily guidance in Luteal. It links every user-facing card on the Today screen and the Duo partner screen to authoritative clinical consensus and patient-reported health outcome frameworks.

## Core principles

1. **Lived experience with clinical rigor.** Medical conditions like endometriosis, PMDD, PCOS, and perimenopausal transitions involve physical pain, neurochemical sensitivity, cognitive fatigue, and systemic disruption. Guidance acknowledges this lived reality without clinical coldness.
2. **Multiple corroborating authorities.** No condition or phase relies on a single isolated study. Advice synthesizes consensus guidelines from French and international bodies:
   - **France:** Haute Autorité de Santé (HAS), Collège National des Gynécologues et Obstétriciens Français (CNGOF), Institut National de la Santé et de la Recherche Médicale (Inserm), Convergences PP.
   - **International:** European Society of Human Reproduction and Embryology (ESHRE), Monash University, American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG), Royal College of Obstetricians and Gynaecologists (RCOG), International Society for Premenstrual Disorders (ISPMD), British Menopause Society (BMS), World Health Organization (WHO), Cochrane Reviews.
3. **Strict non-diagnostic boundaries.** Tips adapt what the app *offers* based on user-declared tracking contexts. They never screen, diagnose, stage, or prescribe medical regimens.
4. **Honest uncertainty and pacing.** Advice offers practical comfort measures, nervous system relaxation, boundary setting, and clinical observation tools.

## Detailed Clinical Evidence Register

### 1. Menstrual Phase (Règles)

#### General Dysmenorrhea & Flow Management
- **Warmth and local relaxation:**
  - *Evidence:* Heat application (40°C) provides pain relief comparable to first-line NSAIDs by reducing uterine muscle ischemia and improving local pelvic blood flow.
  - *Sources:* NHS Period Pain (2026); Cochrane Systematic Review on superficial heat for dysmenorrhea.
  - *Product decision:* `menstrual_warmth` suggests localized heat (water bottle, heat wrap) for uterine cramping.
- **Gentle movement and pelvic circulation:**
  - *Evidence:* Light aerobic activity and pelvic mobility release endorphins, increase pelvic blood flow, and relieve reflex lumbar tension.
  - *Sources:* NHS Period Pain (2026); ACOG Dysmenorrhea Guidelines.
  - *Product decision:* `menstrual_movement` suggests low-intensity walking or gentle stretches without forcing exertion.
- **Hydration and heavy flow context:**
  - *Evidence:* Menstrual fluid loss of >80 mL/cycle increases risk of transient fatigue, orthostatic dizziness, and dehydration.
  - *Sources:* NHS Heavy Periods (2024); FIGO Menstrual Disorders Committee guidelines.
  - *Product decision:* `menstrual_heavy_flow_hydration` encourages steady water intake and mineral balance during heavy bleeding days.
- **Pain escalation and medical consultation:**
  - *Evidence:* Period pain that causes recurrent absenteeism or fails to respond to level-1 analgesics indicates a need for structured clinical evaluation.
  - *Sources:* HAS (2018/2024); CNGOF (2025); NHS (2026).
  - *Product decision:* `menstrual_pain_support` guides users toward healthcare providers when pain limits daily activities, without inferring a specific pathology.

#### Endometriosis Context in Menstruation
- **Pelvic relaxation and pain guarding:**
  - *Evidence:* Endometriosis-related dysmenorrhea often causes secondary pelvic floor hypertension and central sensitization. Diaphragmatic breathing and pelvic floor resting postures down-regulate autonomic stress responses.
  - *Sources:* HAS Recommandations Endométriose (2017/2024); CNGOF / Convergences PP Recommandations Douleurs Pelviennes (2025).
  - *Product decision:* `menstrual_endo_pelvic_rest` suggests resting postures and diaphragmatic breathing to release pelvic guarding during acute pain flares.
- **Radiating pain validation:**
  - *Evidence:* Deep pelvic endometriosis frequently produces referred pain to the sciatic nerve pathways, lumbar spine, and rectum during menses.
  - *Sources:* HAS / CNGOF (2018); WHO Endometriosis Fact Sheet (2025).
  - *Product decision:* `menstrual_endo_radiating_pain` validates logging pain radiating to the lower back, hips, or legs as structured clinical observation data.
- **Inflammatory fatigue and pacing:**
  - *Evidence:* High levels of peritoneal pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-1, IL-6, TNF-alpha) cause profound systemic fatigue that cannot be overcome by willpower.
  - *Sources:* Inserm (2023); CNGOF (2025); Endomind France.
  - *Product decision:* `menstrual_endo_fatigue_pacing` validates systemic fatigue during period days, encouraging guilt-free resting and task prioritization.

### 2. Follicular Phase (Phase Folliculaire)

#### General Follicular Physiology
- **Natural duration variability:**
  - *Evidence:* Follicular phase duration is the primary source of cycle length variation across different people and within the same person over time.
  - *Sources:* Mihm, Gangooly & Muttukrishna (Animal Reproduction Science 2011); Grieger & Norman (J Med Internet Res 2020).
  - *Product decision:* `follicular_varies` and `follicular_no_fixed_day` normalize varying cycle lengths and dispel the 14-day ovulation myth.
- **Gradual energy return:**
  - *Evidence:* Estrogen rises progressively as the dominant follicle develops, supporting gradual increases in energy, verbal recall, and tissue recovery.
  - *Sources:* ACOG (2023); Mihm et al. (2011).
  - *Product decision:* `follicular_energy_rebound` suggests taking advantage of natural energy returns while listening to baseline recovery needs.

#### PCOS (SOPK) Context in Follicular Phase
- **Extended follicular phase normalization:**
  - *Evidence:* Follicular development in PCOS is prolonged due to elevated anti-Müllerian hormone (AMH) and altered LH pulsatility. Delayed ovulation is an established physiological pattern, not an error.
  - *Sources:* Monash University / ESHRE International Evidence-based Guideline for PCOS (2023); WHO PCOS Fact Sheet (2026).
  - *Product decision:* `follicular_pcos_elongation` explains that extended pre-ovulatory phases are typical in PCOS, preventing tracking anxiety.
- **Physical activity for metabolic support:**
  - *Evidence:* Regular resistance training and moderate aerobic exercise improve peripheral insulin sensitivity and metabolic well-being in PCOS, independent of cycle regularity.
  - *Sources:* ESHRE / Monash University (2023); ACOG PCOS Guidelines.
  - *Product decision:* `follicular_pcos_movement` highlights the benefits of strength and steady movement for sustained metabolic energy.
- **Stress and nervous system regulation:**
  - *Evidence:* Autonomic nervous system tone and adrenal androgen production interact with insulin resistance; stress reduction supports overall neuroendocrine balance.
  - *Sources:* ESHRE (2023); Inserm (2024).
  - *Product decision:* `follicular_pcos_stress_care` encourages daily nervous system decompression routines.

#### Thyroid Context in Follicular Phase
- **Morning energy and temperature tracking:**
  - *Evidence:* Overt and subclinical hypothyroidism slows basal metabolic rate, causing pronounced morning fatigue, brain fog, and cold intolerance.
  - *Sources:* Société Française d'Endocrinologie (SFE); American Thyroid Association (ATA); Güngör Semiz (Cureus 2024).
  - *Product decision:* `follicular_thyroid_fatigue` advises pacing morning activities and noting cold sensitivity for medical reviews.

### 3. Ovulatory Phase (Transition Ovulatoire)

#### General Ovulatory Dynamics & Biomarkers
- **Calendar estimate limitations:**
  - *Evidence:* Even in regular 28-day cycles, calendar calculations predict ovulation within a +/-3 day window in under 30% of cycles. True ovulation is verified only retrospectively via sustained thermal shift, ultrasound, or LH surge.
  - *Sources:* Fehring et al. (JOGNN 2006); ACOG Fertility Awareness FAQ (2023).
  - *Product decision:* `ovulatory_not_confirmed` reminds users that calendar estimates are probabilistic windows, pointing to mucus and BBT for physical indicators.
- **Hydration and cervical mucus observations:**
  - *Evidence:* Estrogen stimulates cervical crypts to produce clear, stretchy, lubricative fluid (E-type mucus) with high water content.
  - *Sources:* ACOG (2023); Sensiplan Clinical Handbooks.
  - *Product decision:* `ovulatory_hydration_mucus` explains that adequate hydration supports clear physical observations of cervical mucus transitions.
- **Mittelschmerz (Ovulatory discomfort):**
  - *Evidence:* Follicular rupture and peritoneal irritation from follicular fluid can cause localized, short-lived unilateral pelvic twinges.
  - *Sources:* NHS Periods (2023); ACOG (2023).
  - *Product decision:* `ovulatory_ovulation_pain` explains brief mid-cycle unilateral sensations as common physical observations.

### 4. Luteal Phase (Phase Lutéale)

#### PMS & PMDD (SPM / TDPM) Context
- **Neurochemical sensitivity validation:**
  - *Evidence:* PMDD and severe PMS are caused by an abnormal neurochemical sensitivity in the brain's GABA-A and serotonin receptors to normal fluctuations of progesterone and allopregnanolone, rather than an absolute hormone excess or deficiency.
  - *Sources:* Inserm (2023); ACOG Clinical Practice Guideline No. 7: Management of Premenstrual Disorders (2023); ISPMD Consensus (2023).
  - *Product decision:* `luteal_pmdd_neuro_validation` reassures users that premenstrual mood drops and sensory overwhelm are genuine neurochemical responses, not personal or emotional weakness.
- **Prospective symptom diary gold standard:**
  - *Evidence:* Clinical guidelines require prospective daily symptom tracking across at least two consecutive cycles to confirm premenstrual timing and separate cyclical patterns from chronic distress.
  - *Sources:* ACOG (2023); RCOG Green-top Guideline No. 48; ISPMD (2023); Inserm (2023).
  - *Product decision:* `luteal_diary` reinforces the value of daily logging over 2+ cycles to understand cyclical timing and prepare for doctor visits.
- **Sleep architecture and progesterone:**
  - *Evidence:* Progesterone elevation increases core body temperature by 0.3–0.5°C and alters REM sleep architecture, frequently causing fragmented sleep, vivid dreams, and morning exhaustion in the late luteal phase.
  - *Sources:* ACOG (2023); National Sleep Foundation; British Menopause Society.
  - *Product decision:* `luteal_sleep_routine` encourages a regular, cooling sleep routine and winding down early to counter progesterone-related sleep disruption.
- **Dietary adjustments for physical tension:**
  - *Evidence:* Reducing excess dietary sodium in the second half of the luteal phase lessens aldosterone-mediated fluid retention, breast tenderness (mastodynia), and abdominal bloating. Complex carbohydrates provide sustained glucose and serotonin precursor availability.
  - *Sources:* ACOG Premenstrual Syndrome FAQ (2024); Cochrane Reviews on premenstrual dietary interventions.
  - *Product decision:* `luteal_sodium_bloating` and `luteal_complex_carbs` offer practical dietary context for fluid retention and energy stabilization.
- **Social pacing and boundary setting:**
  - *Evidence:* Cognitive and emotional bandwidth decreases premenstrually in individuals with PMDD. Proactive schedule lightening and boundary setting prevent emotional exhaustion.
  - *Sources:* ISPMD Guidelines; Inserm (2023); Mind UK.
  - *Product decision:* `luteal_pmdd_pacing` suggests reducing avoidable commitments and practicing gentle self-advocacy during high-symptom luteal days.

#### Endometriosis Context in Luteal Phase
- **Non-menstrual pelvic flare-ups:**
  - *Evidence:* Localized prostaglandin release and peritoneal inflammation in endometriosis lesions can peak in the late luteal phase prior to menstruation, causing premature pelvic heaviness, bowel tension, or bladder urgency.
  - *Sources:* HAS (2017/2024); CNGOF (2025); World Endometriosis Research Foundation (WERF).
  - *Product decision:* `luteal_endo_pelvic_tension` validates non-menstrual pelvic aching in the late luteal phase, suggesting gentle warm baths and pelvic de-escalation.

### 5. Transition / Any Phase: Perimenopause (Périménopause)

#### Vasomotor and Cycle Volatility Context
- **Cycle volatility and STRAW+10 normalization:**
  - *Evidence:* The STRAW+10 consensus framework defines the perimenopausal transition by persistent consecutive cycle length differences of 7 days or more, driven by erratic follicular recruitment and fluctuating estradiol levels.
  - *Sources:* Harlow et al., STRAW+10 (Menopause 2012); British Menopause Society (BMS 2023); Inserm (2024).
  - *Product decision:* `perimeno_cycle_fluctuations` explains that cycle length variations of a week or more are typical during perimenopausal transitions.
- **Night sweats, thermoregulation and sleep:**
  - *Evidence:* Estrogen dips narrow the hypothalamus thermoneutral zone, triggering nocturnal vasomotor flushing and sleep fragmentation.
  - *Sources:* British Menopause Society (BMS); HAS Recommandations Ménopause.
  - *Product decision:* `perimeno_vasomotor_sleep` provides actionable sleep environment advice (breathable layered bedding, cool bedroom temperature, limiting late caffeine/alcohol).
- **Long-term bone and vitality baseline:**
  - *Evidence:* Declining estrogen levels affect bone turnover and cardiovascular elasticity. Weight-bearing physical activity and adequate dietary calcium/vitamin D support long-term health.
  - *Sources:* BMS (2023); Inserm; HAS.
  - *Product decision:* `perimeno_vitality_support` encourages strength-building movement and bone-supportive nutrition.
