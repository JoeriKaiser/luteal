# Research Source Register

## Related registers

Physiology, terminology, and data-model sources live in the backend register, `docs/research/SOURCES.md` in the [folicular](https://github.com/JoeriKaiser/folicular) repository (with topic notes linking each source to the schema decision it informs). This register keeps product, content, privacy, clinical guidance, and platform sources.

## Use

Every external source must be tied to a specific product decision. A source is not permission to reproduce clinical language or add diagnosis features. Content derived from a source requires a verification date, publisher date, jurisdiction note, and plain-language scope check.

## Status definitions

- **Candidate:** identified but not yet reviewed for a product decision.
- **Reviewed:** content and publication context verified directly against the primary source.
- **Implemented:** a documented product decision or shipped code cites the verified source.
- **Retired:** no longer current or appropriate.

## Scope limits recorded during review

- The WHO endometriosis fact sheet states a clinical diagnosis may be made from symptoms plus imaging. That is a clinician activity. Luteal records observations and must not present any pattern as diagnostic support.
- The WHO PCOS fact sheet reports up to 70% of affected people are undiagnosed. This is a reason to make long and irregular cycles work well, not a reason to surface screening prompts.
- Premenstrual guidelines (ACOG, ISPMD, RCOG, NHS) require prospective two-cycle symptom diaries evaluated by a clinician to distinguish PMS/PMDD from other causes. Luteal uses TDPM/SPM only as user-selected tracking contexts, never as inferred labels.
- Under French law (Code de la santé publique Art. L. 1111-8 and CNIL/ANS guidance), a standalone personal cycle tracker used by an individual outside a healthcare pathway is not automatically subject to HDS hosting certification, but all cycle data remains GDPR Article 9 health data subject to CNIL's 2024 mobile application recommendations. Keeping Luteal strictly a personal observation tool (never a medical device or clinical care platform) preserves this boundary.
